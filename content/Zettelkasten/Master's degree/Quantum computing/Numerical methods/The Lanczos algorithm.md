The idea of the Lanczos algorithm is to find a method to transform a general matrix in a [[Tridiagonal matrix]] via a transformation, so that from there on the algebraic operations are easier.

Consider a generic matrix $\hat{O}$ and a random vector $\ket{y_1}$.
After the application $\hat{O}\ket{y_1}$ we remove from this new vector the parallel part respect $\ket{y_1}$, we call this residual.

$$ \ket{r_1} = O\ket{y_1} - \braket{y_1|O|y_1}\ket{y_1} = O\ket{y_1} - a_1\ket{y_1} $$

Is easy to see that this residual is orthogonal to $\ket{y_1}$ by definition.
Now we normalize the residual, calling its norm $b_1$, and define:

$$ \ket{y_2} = \frac{\ket{r_1}}{b_1} $$

Again, we do the operation $\hat{O}\ket{y_2}$, this time we remove both the parallel component from $\ket{y_2}$ and $\ket{y_1}$, as the multiplication may have reintroduced them.
So we have:

$$ \ket{r_2} = O\ket{y_2} - \braket{y_1|O|y_2}\ket{y_2} - \braket{y_1|O|y_2}\ket{y_1} = O\ket{y_2} - b_1\ket{y_1} - a_2\ket{y_2} $$

Again one normalize and define:

$$ \ket{y_3} = \frac{\ket{r_2}}{b_2} $$

Going one more step:

$$ \ket{r_3} = O\ket{y_3} - b_1\ket{y_1} - b_2\ket{y_2}- a_3\ket{y_3} $$

This time we observe that $b_1 =0$, as $b_1 = \braket{y_1|O y_3}=\braket{O y_1|y_3} = \braket{r_1 |y_3} + a_1 \braket{y_1 |y_3} = 0$

The clean Lanczos recurrence is

$$
\hat O |y_n\rangle
=
b_{n-1}|y_{n-1}\rangle
+
a_n |y_n\rangle
+
b_n |y_{n+1}\rangle .
$$

The diagonal coefficient is

$$
a_n
=
\langle y_n | \hat O | y_n \rangle .
$$

The next Lanczos vector is obtained from the residual

$$
b_n |y_{n+1}\rangle
=
\hat O |y_n\rangle
-
b_{n-1}|y_{n-1}\rangle
-
a_n |y_n\rangle .
$$

Equivalently,

$$
|y_{n+1}\rangle
=
\frac{
\hat O |y_n\rangle
-
b_{n-1}|y_{n-1}\rangle
-
a_n |y_n\rangle
}{
b_n
},
$$

where

$$
b_n
=
\left\|
\hat O |y_n\rangle
-
b_{n-1}|y_{n-1}\rangle
-
a_n |y_n\rangle
\right\| .
$$

In conclusion, we define the matrix composed by the first $N$ Lanczos vectors $|y_k\rangle$ as

$$
Y
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\vert & \vert & & \vert \\
|y_1\rangle & |y_2\rangle & \cdots & |y_N\rangle \\
\vert & \vert & & \vert
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Since the Lanczos vectors are orthonormal, one has

$$
Y^\dagger Y = \mathbb{1}
$$

The projection of the operator $\hat O$ onto the Krylov subspace generated by these vectors is therefore

$$
T_N
=
Y^\dagger \hat O Y .
$$

In the Lanczos basis, this projected matrix is tridiagonal:

$$
T_N
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_1 & b_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
b_1 & a_2 & b_2 & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & b_2 & a_3 & \ddots & 0 \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & b_{N-1} \\
0 & \cdots & 0 & b_{N-1} & a_N
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
