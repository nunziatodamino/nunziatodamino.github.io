Consider we are considering $N$ identical particles with the same prescription of [[Fermions and Bosons]].

We have seen formally that [[Creation and annihilation operators in II quantization]] per initial definition take, say considering the creation operator, $N$-particle (anti)symmetric sector to the $(N+1)$-(anti)symmetric particle sector according to the statistics (the annihilation operator do of course the opposite).

If one doesn't want to specify necessarily the statistics of a particle can define the Fock space as:

$$  
\mathcal F=\mathcal{H}_0\oplus \mathcal{H}_1\oplus\cdots  
=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_N.  
$$

It is clear that whether we want to work with bosons or fermions, it holds that $\mathcal F_F \subset  \mathcal F$ and $\mathcal F_B \subset  \mathcal F$, so we are just working with more space than necessary.

Photon states must belong to the symmetric sector $\mathcal F_B$, even when viewed inside the enlarged space $\mathcal F$.
Occupation numbers label a basis of $\mathcal F_B$, not of the unrestricted tensor space. A photon occupation-number basis ket is written as:

$$ \ket{\dots , n_{\bar{k}s}, \dots,n_{\bar{k'}s'}, \dots,n_{\bar{k''}s''},\dots} $$

Where this vector represent a state that contains $n_{\bar{k}s}$ number of photons with wavevector $\bar{k}$ and polarization $s$, $n_{\bar{k'}s'}$ number of photons with wavevector $\bar{k'}$ and polarization $s'$, etc. 
A slimmer notation consists in introducing a multi-index $\alpha = (\bar k, s)$ so that we can rewrite simply:

$$ \ket{\dots , n_{\alpha_i}, \dots,n_{\alpha_j}, \dots} $$
