Consider the case in which an atom is immersed in an external electric field $\bar{E}_{ext}(\bar{r},t)$.
Then the atom will have a non-zero electric dipole.

Classically we can think that the electronic cloud deforms around the nucleus, leading to a non-zero distance between the nucleus and the electronic cloud center (hence the dipole).

From a quantum point of view we can evaluate that a perturbation due to the external field leads to a non-zero dipole average in the final state.
