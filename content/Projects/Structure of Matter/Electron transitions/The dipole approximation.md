Consider a system  with the following Hamiltonian:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathscr{H}}
&=\frac{\hat{\dot{q}}^{2}}{2m}+\hat V_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}+\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{rad}}
\\

&=\frac{1}{2m}\left(\hat{\bar{p}}+e\hat{\bar{A}}\right)^2
-k_e\frac{e^2}{\hat r}
+\sum_{\bar{n},s}\hbar\omega_n\hat N_{\bar{n},s}
\\

&=\underbrace{
\frac{\hat{\bar{p}}^{2}}{2m}-k_e\frac{e^2}{\hat r}
}_{\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{mat}}}
+\underbrace{
\frac{e}{m}\hat{\bar{p}}\cdot\hat{\bar{A}}
+\frac{e^2}{2m}\hat{\bar{A}}^{2}
}_{\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{int}}}
+\underbrace{
\sum_{\bar{n},s}\hbar\omega_n\hat N_{\bar{n},s}
}_{\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{rad}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

So that

$$
\hat{\mathscr{H}}
=\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{mat}}
+\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{int}}
+\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{rad}}.
$$

This Hamiltonian describes an electron in a Coulomb potential where this electron is also capable of emitting a radiation.
This Hamiltonian operates on the Hilbert space:

$$
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{atom}}\otimes\mathcal{F}.
$$

Is clear then that $\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{mat}}$ operates only on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{atom}}$, $\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ only on $\mathcal{F}$ ($\mathcal{F}$ock space), while the interaction term is mixed.
In fact, given the expression of $\hat{\bar{A}}$ (In the monochromatic case, see [[Electromagnetic energy with respect to the vector potential in the Coulomb gauge|here]]):

$$
\hat{\bar{A}}(\hat{\bar{r}},t) = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_0 V \omega}}\, \bar{\varepsilon} \left[ e^{i\bar{k}\cdot\hat{\bar{r}}}\hat{a}\,e^{-i\omega t} + e^{-i\bar{k}\cdot\hat{\bar{r}}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\,e^{i\omega t} \right]
$$

Is really better rewritten as:

$$
\hat{\bar{A}}(\hat{\bar{r}},t) = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_0 V \omega}}\, \bar{\varepsilon} \left[ e^{i\bar{k}\cdot\hat{\bar{r}}}\otimes \hat{a}\,e^{-i\omega t} + e^{-i\bar{k}\cdot\hat{\bar{r}}}\otimes \hat{a}^{\dagger}\,e^{i\omega t} \right]
$$

Now lets focus on $\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{int}}$.  Consider that, if we are in the Coulomb gauge, we can prove that $\sum_i[\hat A_i,\hat P_i^{\mathrm{tot}}]=0$, where $\hat{\bar{P}}^{\mathrm{tot}}=\hat{\bar{P}}\otimes\hat{\mathbb 1}_{\mathrm{rad}}$. This is proved [[Proof of exchange of vector potential and momentum using the Coulomb gauge in the context of the dipole approximation|here]]. On $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{atom}}\otimes\mathcal F$, suppressing the radiation identity in $\hat{\bar p}$, this gives $\hat{\bar A}\cdot\hat{\bar p}=\hat{\bar p}\cdot\hat{\bar A}$.
This leads the $\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{int}}$ to be :

$$\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathrm{int}} =
\frac{e}{m}\hat{\bar{A}}\cdot\hat{\bar{p}}
+\frac{e^2}{2m}\hat{\bar{A}}^{2} = \frac{e}{m}\hat{\bar{A}}\cdot\hat{\bar{p}}
+\mathcal{O}(\hat{\bar{A}}^{2}) 
$$

Where we are limiting ourselves only to first order term in the vector potential.
Now we perform the dipole approximation itself.
The main idea is that:

$$ \bar{k}\cdot{\bar{r}} \ll 1 $$

Now considering  $\bar{k}\cdot{\bar{r}} = kr \cos\vartheta \leq kr$, and considering $r \sim a$ (where $a$ is the typical size of an atom) then this condition can be recasted as:

$$ \bar{k}\cdot{\bar{r}} \ll 1 \qquad \iff \qquad \lambda \gg 2\pi a $$

I.e. we are considering that the field generated by the electron has a wavelength bigger than the size of the atom we are considering (given that $a \sim 1 \mathring{A}$, $\lambda$ is generally bigger in the visible range $\lambda \sim 100 \text{nm}$).
If this condition is satisfied (given that $e^{i\bar{k}\cdot\hat{\bar{r}}} \sim \hat{\mathbb{1}}$ ):

$$
\hat{\bar{A}}(\hat{\bar{r}},t) \to \hat{\bar{A}}_{\text{dipole}}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_0 V \omega}}\, \bar{\varepsilon} \left[ \hat{\mathbb{1}}_{\text{atom}}\otimes \hat{a}\,e^{-i\omega t} +  \hat{\mathbb{1}}_{\text{atom}}\otimes \hat{a}^{\dagger}\,e^{i\omega t} \right]
$$

Leading to the effect (As we are in the Coulomb gauge, one can evaluate the electric field using $\bar{E} = -\partial_t \bar A$) that the electric field within this approximation is uniform within the atom (i.e. it does not depend on position).
More formally we can see that the dipole approximation decouples the action of the operator $\hat{A}$ so that before it acts on $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{atom}}\otimes\mathcal{F}$, while after the approximation it acts only on the Fock space.
