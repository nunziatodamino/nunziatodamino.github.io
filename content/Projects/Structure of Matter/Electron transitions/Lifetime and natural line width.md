Say i have a 2 level system, in which I focus myself only in measuring the spontaneously emitted photons. Given that the difference in the levels is fixed and always $\hbar \omega$, i expect that whenever i measure a photon i will measure a photon of this energy.

Nonetheless this is not the case, and we measure experimentally energies with a Lorentzian profile, peaked in the expected photon energy but with a width $\Delta E$.
This is explainable in principle by the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.
Called $\tau$ the mean lifetime for emitting a photon spontaneously, then:

$$ \Delta E \tau \gg \hbar \quad \iff \quad \Delta E  \gg \frac\hbar{\tau}$$

This is called natural (or intrinsic) line width.
Then, in order to make prediction on this linewidth we have to build an [[Phenomenological exponential-decay ansatz|ansatz]] based on the evaluation of the lifetime.
